What wellbeing means

Two ways to see a life going well

Researchers look at wellbeing from two vantage points. One asks how you judge your life when you step back from it. The other asks how you actually feel as you move through your days. Each captures something the other misses.

Evaluative wellbeing

How you judge your life

All things considered, how satisfied are you with your life as a whole?

This is the reflective view: a sense that life is going well, that it has meaning and direction, and that you are moving toward the life you want. It is closest to what most people mean by quality of life, and to the deep satisfaction of a life well lived.

Experienced wellbeing

How you experience your days

Across an ordinary day, do you feel more enjoyment, calm and interest than stress, worry and sadness?

This is the moment-to-moment view: the balance of positive and negative emotions you actually live through. Good days are not free of hard feelings, but they lean toward the positive more often than not.

Thriving means both

Someone can rate their life highly and still feel stretched thin most days, or enjoy their days while feeling stuck in the bigger picture. The two are related but distinct, and they can respond differently to the same circumstances. To thrive is to be deeply satisfied with your life as a whole and to live it through days where positive emotions outweigh negative ones.

The five areas

Where wellbeing is built

Both views of wellbeing are shaped by the same five areas of life. They are connected: strain in one tends to spill into the others, and progress in one makes the rest easier.
